Bullet Beliefs

"If friends were money id rather have four quarters than ninety nine pennies." —Al Capone

His strategic thinking and relationship building are things to learn from. Not condoning crime though lol.

Always agreed with his take here. A great friend >>> 100 acquaintances. Keep a penny or two in your life though. A penny never seems to matter until the day you're short just one cent. Plus, losing a penny hurts your bank much less than a quarter. Choose your pennies and quarters carefully, and understand unlike coins, the currency of friendship can change form and value.

"When you start walking with God, people will stop walking with you. Not because they don't like you, but because you're about to sacrifice things, that they won't sacrifice." —David Goggins, Chief Petty Officer

I am a huge fan of David Goggins, of his story, of his discipline, of his mentality. Though controversial in his tactics, he was a big reason of me becoming more disciplined.

I find oftentimes when I work hard on something, people either a) doubt me b) downplay the effort or outlook to deal with their knack for comfortability or c) flat out don't understand the point. I have wasted a lot of time in the past trying to find people who did not fit these reaction groups or justify my decisions.

It took me a while to realize no one high-fives you when you're preparing for a 100 mile race. They high-five you after you complete it.

"Your older self is counting on you, your younger self is believing in you."

"The magic you are looking for is in the work you are avoiding."

I looked for the magic so many times in life. It never came. And then I had to work so I did and the magic always came.

"Do not confuse movement with progress. Treading in a strong current will keep you in the same spot."

That's true, you can be moving and not actually get anywhere. But sometimes, even when you're not moving forward, when you're treading water in a strong current, you're still building strength.

Your stamina, discipline, and endurance grow. When that current eases, you'll move faster and farther than before, because you've been accelerating the whole time.

Acceleration > Velocity > Displacement.

Focus less on how far you've gone, and more on how much you're growing your capacity to move.

A few beliefs I feel strongly on (formulated from books, personal anecdotes, and my brother)

There are many things in life that are good for me that I hate doing. Some examples: studying in school (certain subjects I liked, certain ones I hated), eating clean (kale tastes so bad to me), prioritizing work over fun, working out after a 14 hour workday. I know a lot of these others don't like doing either. And I may not like doing them, but I still do it. And I sleep well at night knowing I didn't let my softness get in the way of my progression. Same way Mike Tyson slept well at night knowing he's the only one waking up at 4 to run miles.

Automate/habitualize as many things you hate as you can. You have a set amount of willpower every day (if more interested in this I recommend checking out Willpower by Tierney and Baumeister), and the more you spend it debating if you should do something the less stuff you get done. I may hate cardio and a cold shower in the morning, but if I make that habit as tight as brushing my teeth my willpower remains untouched. Spend your precious willpower on the big decisions, not on whether you should brush your teeth.

Temperance is the best anti-corruption medication.

I really believe every high caliber man that falls in any dimension failed to practice temperance.

If you are selfish, be a good person. It's the best way to serve yourself.

Your outer appearance is a walking billboard. You can advertise whatever you want. Dress well, get in shape, take care of your hygiene, if you want to advertise the best version of yourself. The very first layer of a first impression is initial visual impression (your walking billboard). Losing 30 pounds in a year, I felt the difference more in my interactions than my weight.
