Temperance

Apr 2026

Temperance is the best anti-corruption medication.

The corruption I mean here isn't fraud. It's the slow kind, where something you once controlled starts controlling you. When a brilliant person falls, it usually isn't for lack of talent. Somewhere along the way, they stopped practicing temperance.

My version of temperance is simple. I've never had a drink. I've never used a fake ID, been to a bar or a club, smoked anything, or downloaded a dating app. I don't plan to start.

People tend to read this as either boring or extremely disciplined. It's not boring, and the discipline isn't the kind they picture. It doesn't look like white-knuckling my way past temptation every time. It looks like taking the choice off the table.

I know my limits. I've watched brilliant people close to me get taken over by habits that started as harmless, one-off contact. I have no reason to think I'd be the exception. So instead of testing myself, I skip the test.

The logic is really pretty simple, at least in my head. Moderation takes willpower every single time. Abstinence takes it once. Every "no" I've already decided is a decision I never have to make again, and attention I get to spend somewhere else.

I don't feel like I'm missing out, either. You can't miss what you've never had. A Shirley Temple does it for me. A late-night tennis match with my twin brother is as good as a night gets. I'm not carrying a memory of highs that everything else has to compete with. There's no internal negotiation, and that kind of peace feels really really good.

I'm not vice-free. Some things don't have a clean off-switch, and with those, temperance means moderation. I'm still working on it, but wherever I can, I make the choice a non-negotiable.

Some people can genuinely balance these things. I can't, and I'd rather know that about myself than find out the hard way.

If there's something you keep going back to that you wish you didn't, try cutting off contact entirely before you try managing it. No contact means no dependence. No dependence means no desire.

Eliud Kipchoge said, "Only the disciplined ones in life are free." Temperance is how I stay free.
